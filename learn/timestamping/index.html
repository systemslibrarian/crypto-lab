    What should you conclude?

    • Changing a document breaks the match to its signed fingerprint.
    • Changing a signed time breaks signature verification.
    • A dishonest authority can sign a false time with a valid key.
    • A rewritten chain can be internally consistent. A previously published checkpoint can expose the rewrite.

    Try explaining why the third result is compatible with the first two.

    Where trust enters

    RFC 3161 uses signed tokens, a trustworthy time source, policy identifiers and certificate validation. Linked timestamping binds records to earlier commitments; an external publication or witness prevents an operator from quietly replacing the whole history.

    This workshop computes real SHA-256 and P-256 ECDSA. The clock, signer and checkpoint are all local simulations. It creates no independently trusted timestamp, certificate chain, blockchain or interoperable RFC 3161 token. Existence evidence does not by itself establish authorship.
