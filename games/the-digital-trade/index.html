Aim for an exchange in which both sides receive the promised digital items.

What's fair exchange?

Sending your valuable data first lets a dishonest trader vanish. A rule-enforcing third party can coordinate deposits, but now you must trust that service and its enforcement.

Fingerprint check: A SHA-256 digest detects differences between a deposited item and a separately authenticated expected digest. It does not prove legal ownership, identity, value, or whether the listing itself was truthful.

Timeout: In this game's contract, you can reclaim your deposit after two simulated ticks if the swap was never released.

How realistic is this?

It is a state-machine puzzle, not a live marketplace or deployable escrow contract. Both parties, escrow and the clock run in one browser. There is no signature checking, identity verification, blockchain, payment, independent storage, consensus, or dispute resolution. An idealized honest intermediary enforces this game's release/refund rules.

In a purely two-party protocol, neither party can necessarily force the other to complete after receiving what they want. Fair exchange needs additional assumptions such as a trusted intermediary, enforceable timeout, or carefully analyzed blockchain/optimistic-fair-exchange mechanism.
