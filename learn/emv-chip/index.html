Why a chip transaction needs a fresh authenticator

A card authenticates data for a particular checkout. Change the amount or repeat a previously accepted packet and ask the issuer what it would do.

Teaching model: This page computes HMAC-SHA-256 over synthetic fields to show the online authentication idea. It does not implement EMV application cryptograms, chip commands, payment-network rules, offline card authentication, or cardholder verification. Do not enter real card information.

What this experiment establishes

Changing a bound field: the issuer cannot validate the altered packet under the card’s secret. A visible card number alone cannot create the authenticator.

Freshness and replay: a challenge ties the request to a checkout; the model issuer also tracks accepted counters. These are illustrative controls, not a promise that every real EMV deployment uses this exact replay policy.

Actual EMV chip payments distinguish online cryptograms checked remotely by issuers, commonly with symmetric cryptography, from offline authentication that a terminal can verify with public-key cryptography. The latter is outside this model. Tokenizing a stored card number, as in Format Ward, solves a different problem.
