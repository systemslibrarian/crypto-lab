What this teaches

Independent committed randomness prevents either participant from freely rewriting their seed after learning the other player's choice.

Important limit: This is a locally simulated card battle and post-game audit—not a complete secure mental-poker protocol. The browser knows both seeds and both hands. Real remote mental poker needs private dealing, verifiable shuffles, authentication, and abort handling.

How does the shuffle work?

The game combines two secret hexadecimal seeds with SHA-256, then uses a deterministic Fisher–Yates shuffle with SHA-256-derived draws and rejection sampling. This is a transparent educational construction, not a standard cryptographic shuffle or a validated random-number generator. The audit independently recomputes the committed opening and deck after play.

Where this leads

From a sealed shuffle to verifiable protocols

Your SHA-256 audit can detect a changed opening, but it cannot by itself guarantee private card dealing, an authenticated opponent, or a verifiable shuffle. Explore the individual building blocks.
